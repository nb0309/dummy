# WCAG 2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap — design notes

Why this criterion is built the way it is. The mechanics live in
[`generator/README.md`](../generator/README.md); this document records the reasoning,
including the two places where the obvious design would have been wrong.

## Why it needed a probe at all

2.1.2 was the one criterion the codebase already knew it was missing, and said so in
three places:

- `focusOrderProbe` set a `stalled` flag "so the evidence can attribute it to 2.1.2
  rather than to 2.4.3";
- `src/evidence.py` rendered the stall as "WCAG 2.1.2's question, not 2.4.3's";
- `src/skills/focus_order_2_4_3.md` said "that is 2.1.2 (No Keyboard Trap), **which
  has no skill here yet**".

So a trap was already being detected, deliberately excluded from 2.4.3, and then
dropped on the floor. Nothing classified it.

The tempting cheap fix was a skill with no probe — judge 2.1.2 from `element_html`
plus the existing `sr_focus_order.stalled` flag. That cannot work, for a reason worth
stating plainly: **escapability is not a property of markup.** A `keydown` handler
that calls `preventDefault()` on Tab and one that calls it only for `Ctrl+Tab` are two
characters apart in source and opposite in outcome. Whether focus can leave has to be
established by trying to leave.

## The central insight: containment is not the defect

This is the criterion where the obvious signal is the wrong one, and getting it
backwards would have made the feature worse than useless.

A modal dialog is **supposed** to cycle Tab within itself while it is open. That is
the ARIA authoring practice, not a bug. A grid, a tree, a text editor are all entitled
to consume Tab for their own purposes. What 2.1.2 forbids is holding focus with **no
way out**:

> If keyboard focus can be moved *to* a component using a keyboard, then focus can be
> moved *away* from that component using only a keyboard — and if that requires more
> than unmodified arrow or Tab keys, the user is advised of the method.

A probe that stopped at "focus is looping" would therefore flag **every correctly
built dialog on the web**. Detecting the loop is the easy half and the worthless half.

Everything else in the design follows from this. The loop detector is phase 1; the two
phases that try to *get out* are what actually decide the criterion.

## Three phases, and what each one rules out

| phase | key | rules out |
|---|---|---|
| 1 forward | `Tab` | nothing is holding focus at all |
| 2 reverse | `Shift+Tab` | a component you can simply back out of |
| 3 escape | `Escape`, then `Tab` | the conforming dialog pattern |

Phase 2 exists because "focus can be moved away" does not specify a direction. A
widget that swallows forward Tab but releases Shift+Tab is escapable with an
unmodified key, and calling that a trap would be wrong on the text of the SC.

Phase 3 is the one that earns its keep. Two of the ten fixtures —
`modal-wraps-tab-but-escape-closes` and `grid-advises-escape-to-leave` — are
**contained**, with the same containment as the fixtures that fail, and both conform.
Without phase 3 they are indistinguishable from a trap. That pair is the whole reason
the phase exists, and they exist in the suite specifically to hold that line, in the
same spirit as 2.4.6's `repeated-link-text-same-destination`.

"Got out" means focus reached `<body>` **or** any element outside the trapped set.
Leaving the *component* is what the SC asks for, not leaving the page.

## Why `cap` is an abstention, not a finding

The forward sweep resolves into four outcomes: `escaped`, `stalled`, `cycled`, `cap`.
Three are informative; `cap` means the sweep hit its limit without either leaving the
page or repeating a stop.

It is tempting to read that as "focus never got out, therefore trapped". It is not:
**a page with more tabbable elements than the cap produces exactly this signature.**
So `cap` is rendered as inconclusive and the skill is told to return
`insufficient_evidence`. This mirrors how `truncated` is handled for 2.4.3 and 1.3.2 —
a partial observation is not a weak finding, it is not a finding.

## The advisory clause, and two bugs it exposed

The second half of the SC — "the user is advised of the method" — is what makes a
non-Tab exit legitimate. So the probe captures `region`: the enclosing widget's
`role`/`aria-modal`, its `aria-keyshortcuts`, its `aria-describedby` text, and
`advisory`, the region's text minus every control's text.

Both details there were wrong in the first implementation, and both were caught by
running against the fixtures rather than by reading the code:

**1. The region resolved too low.** The nearest common ancestor of the trapped stops
is the natural choice, and it is wrong. For a single pinned grid cell that ancestor is
the `role="row"` one level up, while the instruction is authored on the `role="grid"`
above it. The pass fixture's advisory came back as `"11 12 13 14"` — the sibling day
buttons — and the decisive evidence for the one fixture that turns on the advisory
clause was silently missing. Fixed by continuing the walk to the nearest enclosing
*widget* role.

**2. Control text drowned the advisory.** Excluding only the *trapped* elements' text
left every other control in. The failing and passing calendars both advised
`"11 12 13 14"`. Fixed by excluding all interactive descendants, after which the
failing grid advises nothing and the passing one advises
`"Use the arrow keys to choose a date, then press Escape to leave the calendar."`

Both bugs had the same shape: the probe ran, produced plausible output, and destroyed
the single signal it existed to capture. Neither would have been visible without a
pass fixture built to depend on it.

## Why this evidence section leads with a verdict

Every other page-level section in `src/evidence.py` deliberately **withholds** one.
2.4.3 renders its comparisons as "observations" and states that a clean result is not
a pass; 2.4.6 does the same for duplicates; 1.3.2 reports which visual ordering the
reading order matches and then hands over the question of whether order carries
meaning. The reasoning is consistent: those criteria turn on something no measurement
reaches — whether an order preserves *meaning*, whether a heading is *descriptive*. A
keyword list would only fake that judgement.

2.1.2 splits differently, and the split is what justifies breaking the pattern:

- **"Did any key move focus out"** is a measurement. Not a judgement, not a heuristic,
  not a proxy. The ladder pressed the keys and recorded the answer.
- **"Does this text tell the user how to leave"** is genuinely semantic, and stays
  with the model.

So the mechanical half is stated outright, and stated **first**. That ordering is not
cosmetic. In probe order, a conforming modal renders as:

```text
- Focus is LOOPING over 3 components and never reaches the end of the page: …
- Shift+Tab did NOT move focus out either — both unmodified Tab directions are blocked.
- The ESCAPE key released focus … There IS a keyboard way out.
```

Three alarming lines, then the one that exonerates it — and an opening identical to a
real trap's. A reader accumulating alarm down that list has every reason to flag a
correctly built dialog. The fix was to lead with a single `ESCAPABLE` /
`NOT ESCAPABLE` resolution that names the key that worked, adds that containment is
the *required* pattern when the region is a dialog, and says explicitly not to flag
2.1.2 for the hold described underneath. The alarm words in the supporting lines were
neutralised at the same time ("blocked" → "held", "never reaches the end of the page" →
"does not advance past them") — same facts, no implied verdict.

The rubric was updated to agree with it, since evidence and skill disagreeing is its
own failure mode: the skill now says the first observation is decisive for the
mechanical half and not to let the loop, the stall or the blocked Shift+Tab talk you
out of it.

## Why the probe runs last

It is the only page-level probe that **mutates the page** — Escape legitimately closes
a dialog. `capture.mjs` had a standing guarantee that page probes are read-only, which
is what let them run before the per-sample transcripts. Phase 3 breaks it.

Rather than weaken the guarantee, the capture loop now buffers per-sample transcripts
and runs `keyboardTrapProbe()` after them. A transcript read after an Escape would
describe a different page from the one whose HTML was snapshotted — the row would be
internally inconsistent, and nothing downstream could detect it.

The row-buffering change touches every suite, so it was verified by re-capturing 2.4.3
and 3.3.2 and diffing against the committed datasets: zero differing rows on
`element_html`, `parent_html`, `sr_transcript`, `sr_focus_order` and
`sr_label_instruction`. The same diff covers the `readActiveStop` extraction, which
was shared out of `focusOrderProbe` — its key order is preserved deliberately, because
that order is what `JSON.stringify` writes into the dataset column.

## Known limits

- **The key ladder is fixed.** Tab, Shift+Tab, Escape. A widget escapable only by
  `Ctrl+M` reads here as a trap, which is why `aria-keyshortcuts` is captured beside
  the result and the skill is told to abstain rather than flag when a key it never
  pressed is advertised.
- **Framed content.** `activeElement` in the top document is the `<iframe>` element,
  so a trap *inside* a frame cannot be distinguished from a stall *on* it.
- **`cap`.** See above — an abstention by construction.
- **Fixtures need `<script>`.** No declarative markup can block Tab, so unlike the
  other page-level suites every trap here is a handler. The consequence for fixture
  authoring: the self-annotation must be an **HTML comment**, which `normalize.js`
  strips. A `//` comment inside `<script>` is not stripped and would hand the model
  the verdict inside its own evidence.

## Status

Mechanically complete and verified: the probe produces the intended outcome on all ten
fixtures, the leading observation maps 1:1 onto ground truth, all 79 rows across all
eight datasets render and route, and the keyboard-trap section stays confined to 2.1.2
rows while the other three page probes stay off them.

**Unverified:** the model's own accuracy. `python -m src.run --input 2.1.2_dataset.csv`
needs `AZURE_OPENAI_ENDPOINT`, which was not available when this was built, so recall
on the five fails and false positives on the five passes are unmeasured. The ordering
work above makes a false positive less likely; it does not prove one will not happen.
If the two contained-but-conforming rows still come back `inaccessible`, the next lever
is `BASE_SYSTEM_INSTRUCTIONS` in `src/classifier.py`, which currently carves out 4.1.3
as the one criterion where silence is meaningful and says nothing about criteria where
an alarming signal is expected behaviour.

## An unrelated finding, recorded here because it was found here

The existing page-level suites **leak their ground truth**. For a `<body>` sample
`parent_html` is the `<html>` element, which carries the `<head>` — including
`<title>Send us a message - Failing Example</title>`. The model can read the verdict
off its own evidence in 2.4.3, 2.4.6 and 1.3.2.

The 2.1.2 fixtures were given neutral titles for this reason. Fixing the other three
suites means retitling their fixtures and recapturing, and was left alone as separate
work.
